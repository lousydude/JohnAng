Macro brief · SMU Emerging Markets · January 2026 · Sole author

Colombia: positioned for growth through stability

Thesis. Colombia's recovery is domestically driven and services-led, which makes it one of Latin America's more stable consumption stories and a supportive backdrop for FMCG companies.

>65%

of GDP from services: growth tracks household demand, not commodity prices

11% → 9–9.5%

unemployment, 2023 to 2025, driven by job creation in services and logistics

13% → ~6%

inflation, early 2023 to late 2025, turning real wage growth positive

How the argument runs

  1. Growth composition. Unlike copper-exposed Chile or extractives-heavy Peru, Colombia's output is dominated by retail, financial services, transport and business services, which smooths growth through commodity cycles.
  2. Labour market. Policies that cut non-wage hiring costs and encouraged formalisation helped private-sector job creation, particularly at SMEs.
  3. Real incomes. Faster disinflation than Brazil or Mexico lifted household purchasing power, feeding spending on food, beverages and everyday goods.
  4. So what for FMCG. Employment growth plus positive real wages supports sustained volume growth for consumer staples.

Sources used: IMF, DANE national accounts, Banco de la República, World Bank Colombia Update, Trading Economics, Bloomberg. The full brief is internal club research and is available on request.
